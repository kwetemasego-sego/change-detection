A hectare (ha) is 10,000 m²: a square 100 m long on each side, a bit bigger than a football pitch.
How are small changes ignored?

Each 10 m square gets a plant greenness score (NDVI) and a built-up score (NDBI), both from −1 to +1. A square only counts as changed if its plant score moved by 0.15 or more, or its built-up score rose by 0.10 or more. Smaller shifts usually come from haze, the sun's angle or the season, not from real changes on the ground.

A changed square also needs at least 2 neighbours that changed the same way, because real changes cover a patch of ground. Water and shorelines, ground that was wet before, and ground that fell into a new shadow are left out, because tides and the sun make them look different without anything changing.
